# Age UK Bury: Data Sources for Prospect Population

## Executive Summary

This document outlines legitimate, compliant data sources for populating Age UK Bury's prospect database with older adults (50+) geographically targeted to the Bury borough. All sources comply with UK GDPR and charity data governance requirements.

---

## PRIMARY DATA SOURCES

### 1. **Bury Council - Adult Social Care & Health Services**

**Availability:** High (Direct Partnership)  
**Compliance:** GDPR-compliant under care partnership agreement  
**Data Points:** Age, location (postcode/ward), support needs flagged

**How to Access:**
- Direct relationship via Bury Integrated Care Partnership (ICP) and Place-Based Primary Care Commissioning Committee
- Formal Data Sharing Agreement (DSA) required
- Contact: Bury Council ASC Lead / Adult Services Commissioner

**Target Data:**
- Adults 50+ in Bury requiring social care assessment
- Housing assistance applicants (age 65+)
- Social prescribing referrals from GPs
- Bury population: ~73,600 people aged 50+ (2021 Census, ~38% of population)

**Strengths:**
- Geographically precise (by ward/postcode sector)
- High likelihood of support needs alignment
- Low cost/free under partnership
- Directly reflects local demographics

**Weaknesses:**
- Requires formal governance agreement
- Data may be limited to active social care contacts
- Annual refresh cycles typical

---

### 2. **NHS Bury Integrated Care Board (ICB) - Social Prescribing**

**Availability:** Medium-High (Existing Partnership)  
**Compliance:** GDPR-compliant under NHS data sharing protocols  
**Data Points:** Age, GP surgery location, presenting health/social needs

**How to Access:**
- Already established pathway: Age UK Bury is likely part of Bury ICB's social prescribing network
- Direct referrals from GPs via social prescribing link workers
- Formal Data Processing Agreement (DPA) with NHS England framework

**Process:**
- GPs refer patients 50+ to social prescribing services
- Link workers identify candidates suitable for Age UK services
- Bury locality plan 2025-26 includes commitment to social prescribing expansion

**Strengths:**
- Warm handoff (patient consent already captured)
- High intent pool (referred specifically for support)
- Integrated into NHS pathways nationally
- Covers preventative to high-needs tiers

**Weaknesses:**
- Referral-based (not proactive outreach)
- Dependent on GP engagement
- Limited demographic targeting capability

---

### 3. **GP Patient Data Sharing - England-wide Initiative**

**Availability:** Medium (New, 2024-2025 rollout)  
**Compliance:** GDPR opt-out model (patients can object)  
**Data Points:** Age, medical needs, social determinants of health

**How to Access:**
- NHS England has enabled coded GP data sharing with charities serving older people
- Bury GPs (federated through Bury GP Federation) can provide anonymized patient lists
- Patient consent via opt-out notice to GP practices

**Partnership Pathway:**
- Age UK Bury and Bury GP Federation to establish data sharing agreement
- Quarterly/semi-annual extracts of patients 50+ with identified support needs
- Anonymized but age/postcode-banded data available

**Strengths:**
- Largest available dataset (all GP-registered patients in Bury)
- Captures breadth of population (not just high-needs)
- Opt-out model = presumed consent
- Cost-effective

**Weaknesses:**
- Anonymized initially (no direct identifiers)
- Patient objection rights must be respected
- Data lag (typically 3-6 months)

---

### 4. **Electoral Roll - Public Access (with Caveats)**

**Availability:** Low (Limited utility, but compliant)  
**Compliance:** GDPR-compliant under public register exemption  
**Data Points:** Name, address, voter registration status

**How to Access:**
- Electoral Commission publishes redacted electoral roll
- Bury Borough Council maintains full register (available with formal request)
- Data brokers can provide compiled, postal-coded lists

**Legal Status:**
- Electoral roll is a public register under GDPR recital 23
- Organizations using electoral roll must have lawful basis:
  - **Legitimate interest** (limited list use for targeted outreach)
  - **Legal obligation** (licensing, statutory requirements)
  - **Performance of task in public interest** (charity mission)

**Practical Limitations:**
- **No age data on public electoral roll** (cannot legally filter by DOB)
- Can only filter by postcode/area
- Requires manual age verification or append services
- High false positive rate for age targeting

**When to Use:**
- Outreach campaigns to specific wards/postcode areas
- Validation of existing prospect lists
- **NOT recommended** as primary source due to age data gap

---

### 5. **Data Brokers & Age-Targeted Lists**

**Availability:** High (Paid Service)  
**Compliance:** Variable—requires due diligence  
**Cost:** £500-£5,000 annually depending on segmentation

**Reputable UK Brokers:**
- **DataBubble.co.uk** - GDPR-compliant B2B lists, age segmentation available
- **Experian/Mosaic** - Lifestyle segmentation, supports non-profit targeting
- **Royal Mail Postcode Address File (PAF)** - Geographic segmentation

**What's Available:**
- Residential address lists (postcode + age band: 50-64, 65-74, 75+)
- Enhanced with lifestyle indicators (living alone, retired status inferred)
- Bury-specific extracts (Bury postcode sectors: BL8, BL9, BL10, M25-9)

**Sourcing:**
- Data brokers aggregate from: Electoral roll, council tax records, consumer data, lifestyle surveys
- All sources must have GDPR lawful basis (usually consent or legitimate interest)
- Brokers should provide compliance audit trail

**Strengths:**
- Age-segmented data available
- Geographic precision (postcode level)
- Rapid deployment (1-2 weeks)
- Validated contact details

**Weaknesses:**
- Cost barrier for smaller campaigns
- Requires GDPR audit of broker (compliance responsibility on Age UK Bury)
- Not locally specific to known local needs
- High unsubscribe rates from cold contact

**Compliance Requirement:**
- Only use brokers who provide DPA certification
- Verify broker's data sourcing methodology
- Document compliance decision in privacy documentation

---

### 6. **Bury Council Housing Register & Council Tax**

**Availability:** Medium (Direct Request)  
**Compliance:** GDPR-compliant under Data Subject Access or partnership agreement  
**Data Points:** Age, address, housing status, income (limited)

**How to Access:**
- Council Tax Data: Available via Data Sharing Agreement with Bury Council
- Captures owner-occupiers, private renters, council tenants
- Annual data refresh typical

**Target Segments:**
- Households headed by 65+ (living alone or couple)
- Council-maintained housing stock (more likely to need support)

**Strengths:**
- Comprehensive UK coverage (90% of population on council tax)
- Identifies owner-occupiers (higher deprivation screening)
- Linked to council data = fraud prevention partnerships established

**Weaknesses:**
- Council tax bands don't equal income/need
- Age filters require additional processing
- Not real-time (annual updates)

---

### 7. **Bury-Specific Community Lists & Partners**

**Availability:** High (Existing Networks)  
**Compliance:** GDPR-compliant (internal networks)  
**Data Points:** Contact details, known needs, interests

**Existing Relationships:**
- **Bury Councillors & Ward Teams** - Maintain constituent records, can flag older residents
- **Bury Parish Councils** - Community networks, local knowledge
- **Bury Libraries** - Older adults user base, book club members
- **Bury Leisure Services** - Gym/activities participants 50+
- **Bury Volunteer Centre** - Volunteer databases include demographic data

**How to Access:**
- Formal information sharing agreements already in place
- Annual data refresh cycles typical
- Volunteer consent already captured

**Strengths:**
- Warm intros (existing community trust)
- Local knowledge of needs
- Opt-in audiences (high engagement intent)

**Weaknesses:**
- Fragmented across multiple systems
- Requires manual consolidation
- Limited scope compared to broader population
